Both of the kit's accents are named after pigments people have made for centuries. Vermilion is a red that leans toward orange, the red of Swiss posters and railway signs. Verdigris is the blue-green that copper turns as it weathers on old roofs and statues.

## One accent, used rarely

A site picks one and keeps it. The accent marks the one thing that matters on a screen: the main action, the current item, a switch that is on. Used everywhere, it would stop meaning anything.

That is also why it comes in three strengths:

- `accent` for large shapes and the focus ring.
- `accent-text` for small text on the cream page, darker so it passes contrast.
- `accent-bright` for small text on the dark bars, lighter for the same reason.

## Why not more

A second accent would need its own rules for when to use which, and every page would have to follow them. One accent needs no rules beyond "rarely".

> Color is rare. That is what lets it mean something when it appears.

Both accents pass the same contrast checks, in light and dark. Try the gear on the right edge of this page to see the other one.
